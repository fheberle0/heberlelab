The Best Juicy Skillet Pork Chops

Pork 45 min

Adapted from Inspired Taste (Adam and Joanne Gallagher)

Ingredients

  • 4 pork chops, about 1-inch thick and 6–7 oz (170–200g) each
  • to taste salt
  • 1 tbsp 8 g all-purpose flour
  • 1 tsp chili powder
  • 1 tsp garlic powder
  • 1 tsp onion powder
  • 1/2 tsp smoked paprika
  • 1/2 tsp ground black pepper
  • 1 tbsp 15 ml neutral oil (avocado or vegetable)

Pan sauce

  • 1 cup 240 ml low-sodium chicken stock
  • 1 tbsp 15 ml apple cider vinegar
  • 2 tsp honey or brown sugar
  • 1 tbsp 14 g butter
  • 2 tbsp fresh parsley, chopped (optional)

Method

  1. Season the pork chops on both sides with salt (just under 1/4 tsp per chop) and let rest 30 minutes at room temperature.
  2. Meanwhile, mix the flour, chili powder, garlic powder, onion powder, smoked paprika, and black pepper in a small bowl.
  3. Pat the pork chops dry with a paper towel, then rub both sides with the spice mix.
  4. Heat the oil in a skillet with a lid over medium-high heat. When shimmering, add the pork and cook until golden, 2–3 minutes.
  5. Flip. If there's a fattier edge, hold the chops fat-side-down with tongs until it sizzles and browns slightly, about 30 seconds.
  6. Reduce heat to low, cover, and cook 6–12 minutes until an instant-read thermometer reads 63°C (145°F) at the thickest part. Check at 5 minutes, then every 2 minutes after. Without a thermometer, the chops are done when the juices run clear.
  7. Transfer to a plate, cover loosely with foil, and rest 5 minutes.
  8. For the pan sauce: increase heat to medium-high and add the chicken stock, vinegar, and honey. Simmer until reduced by half, scraping up the browned bits with a wooden spoon.
  9. Taste and adjust with more salt, vinegar, or honey. Off the heat, once the sauce stops simmering, swirl in the butter.
  10. Return the pork chops to the pan and spoon the sauce over them (or slice first). Scatter with parsley and serve.

Notes

  • Thinner or thicker chops work too — thinner cook faster, so watch the thermometer closely. Bone-in chops add a minute or two of cooking time.
  • Any spice rub works as a substitute, as long as it's salt-free.
  • Gluten-free: the flour can be skipped — the crust is less even, but rubbing the spices directly on the pork still tastes great.
  • Variation: after removing the chops, sauté sliced onion, apple, and a little fresh thyme until soft, then continue with the stock, vinegar, and honey for an onion-apple pan sauce.
