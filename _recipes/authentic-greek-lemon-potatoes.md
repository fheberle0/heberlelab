steps:
  - text: Soak the potato wedges in a bowl of cold water for 30 minutes. This draws out excess starch so they crisp up properly.
    timer_minutes: 30
  - Preheat the oven (with convection if available) to 200°C (400°F).
  - Drain the potatoes and pat thoroughly dry with a clean towel.
  - Combine the dried potatoes with the olive oil, oregano, 3 tbsp lemon juice, garlic, cloves, allspice, salt, and pepper in a large bowl. Mix well by hand until every piece is coated.
  - Spread the potatoes in a single layer in a roasting pan, scraping in any oil left in the bowl. Don't pile them up.
  - Pour the hot water into one corner of the pan, tilting so it spreads underneath. Never pour directly over the potatoes — it washes off the oil.
  - text: Roast at 200°C (400°F) until the liquid begins to boil, about 10–15 minutes.
    timer_minutes: 12
  - text: Reduce the oven to 160°C (320°F) and continue roasting 45–60 minutes, stirring every 30 minutes and adding a splash more hot water if the pan looks dry. Done when fork-tender but still holding their shape, with golden crispy edges.
    timer_minutes: 50
  - Once out of the oven, squeeze fresh lemon juice over the top to taste, starting with half a lemon.
  - Taste and add a little more salt if needed. Serve immediately while hot and crisp.
notes:
  - "Potato variety in Sweden: King Edward (closest to Yukon Gold), Asterix, or Folva all work well — avoid mandelpotatis and other mjölig (floury) varieties, which fall apart during the long roast."
  - The 30-minute cold-water soak is essential for texture — don't skip it.
  - Smaller wedges get crunchier than long ones.
  - Pairs traditionally with roasted chicken or lamb so the potatoes soak up the meat juices.
