steps:
  - section: Cake
    text: Heat the oven to 175°C (350°F). Butter both 23cm (9") pans and dust with cocoa powder instead of flour, so there's no white residue on the finished cake.
  - section: Cake
    text: In a stand mixer on low, combine the sugar, flour, cocoa, baking powder, baking soda, and salt.
  - section: Cake
    text: In a separate bowl, whisk the eggs, milk, oil, and vanilla. Add to the dry mixture on low, then medium speed, mixing only until it comes together.
  - section: Cake
    text: Add the hot coffee by hand with a spatula — the batter is thin at this stage and the mixer will throw it out of the bowl. Stir just until incorporated.
  - section: Cake
    text: Divide evenly between the two pans. Bake 30–35 minutes, until a skewer comes out with a few moist crumbs.
    timer_minutes: 35
  - section: Cake
    text: Cool in the pans for 10 minutes, then turn out onto a rack and cool completely. The layers must be at room temperature before frosting or the buttercream will slump.
  - section: Mint frosting
    text: Beat the powdered sugar, butter, and mint extract in a stand mixer on medium until light and fluffy.
  - section: Mint frosting
    text: Add the milk one tablespoon at a time, stopping as soon as the frosting spreads easily with a spatula — the amount needed varies with butter temperature.
  - section: Mint frosting
    text: Add the green food colouring one drop at a time on medium speed until you reach the shade you want.
notes:
  - "This frosting is scaled to 2/3 of the original recipe (which is written for cupcake quantities) — still a comfortable amount for filling, covering, and a little piping."
  - Mint extract intensity varies by brand — if the mint level was right last time, err generous.
  - Milk is a range, not a fixed amount — treat it as a texture adjustment, added to feel rather than measured exactly.
  - Butter should be at room temperature and dent when pressed, not greasy or melting — cold butter leaves lumps that never fully beat out at this size.
  - Frosting freezes well for up to 3 months in an airtight container. Thaw in the fridge overnight, then re-whip briefly to bring the texture back.
